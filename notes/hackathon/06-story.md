# Duet presentation script

Drafted 2026-09-19 for the 3:30 PM demo. About two minutes of talk over the seven cards, then walk to the board.

## Should the diner story go in?

Yes, as the close, not the open.

- Card 1's thesis is already a strong cold open. A minute of story in front of it delays the product.
- The idea really did grow in this order: sketch with a robot, then Claude, then the artists, then the father. Told honestly, the story ends on the diner, so the diner belongs at the end.
- Card 7 is a black card with "One person. Two days." on it. It is the quiet moment of the deck, and it stays up while the arm draws. Telling the diner story there means the audience watches the demo as the other side of that table.
- The idea's evolution ("yesterday I thought it would be fun to sketch with a robot...") goes on card 2, because its three steps are exactly card 2's three ingredients: robot, Claude, artist.

No deck change needed. The story is spoken.

## Script

**Card 1. Thesis.** Say nothing before the first line. One line per advance.

> Anyone can now study with the greatest minds in history.
> *[advance]* Nobody could make art with them.
> *[advance]* Until today.

**Card 2. What Duet is.**

> Yesterday morning I thought it would be fun to sketch with a robot. Then I thought, what if Claude was the one deciding what to draw next. And then, what if it drew like Van Gogh, or Mondrian, or Keith Haring.
>
> That's Duet. You make a mark. It looks, understands, and answers, in the hand of an artist you choose.

**Card 3. One turn.**

> This is a real turn from last night. Someone drew this. Claude looked at it and said:
>
> *[read the bubble, or let them read it]* "A crowded world of creatures, flowers and dancing figures. A small green dancing figure in the open lower-right space to balance the crowd."
>
> Eight seconds to look and decide, and then the arm drew this.

**Card 4. Co-creation.**

> Ten exchanges later, this is what came out. Two artists, and one of them was a robot. Everyone who sits down leaves with a piece they made with a partner.

**Card 5. Learning.**

> And you pick up the artist's language, because you're answering it. Haring turns your blob into a figure. Mondrian runs your edges out to a grid. Van Gogh streams dashes around your mark.
>
> You don't study the technique. You have a conversation in it.

**Card 6. How it works.**

> Under the hood it's Viam at every step. The wrist camera photographs the board, Claude reads it and decides, the artist's style turns that into strokes, and the motion service draws them safely around the table.

**Card 7. The build.** This card stays up for the demo.

> One person. Two days. Claude and Viam.
>
> *[beat]*
>
> One more thing, because I only figured this out halfway through.
>
> When I was a kid, my father used to take me to the diner. He was an architect, so he always had a pen, and while we waited for the food we'd draw together. He'd draw something, I'd draw on top of it, he'd draw again, until the food came and the drawing was done. That's how I got into art.
>
> I didn't walk in here thinking about any of that. But somewhere in the middle of building this, it hit me. I'd built the other side of that diner table.
>
> *[beat]*
>
> So. Draw one mark, and Duet answers.

*[walk to the board]*

## Timing and cuts

Read at a natural pace this runs about 2:20. Card 7 is about 35 seconds. If you need to lose time, in this order:

1. Card 3: don't read the bubble aloud, point at it and say "Claude said this."
2. Card 6: one sentence. "Viam under every step: camera, Claude, strokes, motion service."
3. Card 5: drop the three artist lines, keep the two sentences around them.

Do not cut card 7.

Optional family line, after "That's how I got into art": "I grew up in a house where everyone made things. My father designed buildings, my mother designed clothes, my brother makes films." Recommended cut in the pitch, because the close is about your father and the table, and two more people in the last twenty seconds split the focus. It stays in the written version below.

## One paragraph (submission form, Discord, gallery)

Duet is a robot arm that draws with you. You make a mark on a whiteboard, Claude looks at a photo of the board and says what it sees and what it will add, and a Viam-controlled xArm6 draws the answer in the hand of an artist you choose: Van Gogh, Mondrian or Keith Haring. You take turns until the drawing is done, and everyone leaves with a piece they made with a partner. I came to the Fine Motor Skills hackathon thinking it would be fun to sketch with a robot. Somewhere in the middle of building it, I realized where the idea came from. As a kid I used to draw with my father, an architect, at the diner, trading lines back and forth until the food came, and that is how I got into art. Duet is the other side of that table.
